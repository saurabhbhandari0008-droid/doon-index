Maldevta, Dehradun: How to Get There, What to Do and Where to Eat

Maldevta is about 13 km from the Clock Tower, where the Song river meets the Sahastradhara stream. How to get there, the canal walk, food shacks and stays.

Maldevta is the riverside valley east of Dehradun where the Song river and the stream from Sahastradhara meet. It's about 13 km from the Clock Tower and around 18 km from ISBT, and on a free afternoon most of the city seems to know the way. On weekends the river banks fill up with families, and Maggi and momo shacks open along the water.

How to get to Maldevta

Take Sahastradhara Road out of the city and turn towards Raipur, then follow the road to Maldevta. By car it's 40 to 45 minutes from ISBT, a little less from the Clock Tower if traffic is kind. Shared autos and local buses run from Dehradun, but a scooter or a cab is easier if you want to stop along the way.

The newer direct road from Dehradun to Dhanaulti also passes through Maldevta.

What to do at Maldevta

Sit by the river. That's the honest answer for most visitors. The Song is wide and shallow in most months, and people spread a sheet under the trees and stay the afternoon. Don't go into the water after rain, when the river rises fast.

The canal walk is the part most guides miss. Dehradun's old canal system still runs here. The Kalanga canal takes water from the Song, the Sahastradhara stream and the Bandal river, and a short detour off the main road brings you to the aqueduct where the canal crosses the Sahastradhara stream. Proby Cautley, the British engineer who later worked on the Upper Ganga Canal at Haridwar, built the first aqueduct on the Kalanga canal in the 1840s. You can walk across it and follow the canal to Do Naali, where the channels from Sahastradhara and the Song meet. Do Naali is busy on weekends and has its own tea and Maggi stalls.

Near the bridge there's an old water mill (gharat) that locals brought back into use a few years ago.

The temple by the canal gives the place its name. One local explanation is that "mal" means goods, because the valley sat on an old trade route.

If you like birds, come early. The river edge and the fields on either side are good for a slow morning walk with binoculars.

Where to eat

Don't expect restaurants. Maldevta is a place for masala Maggi, momos, bread pakoda and chai from the riverside shacks, and that's most of the fun. If you want a proper meal, eat on Sahastradhara Road on the way back, or carry a packed lunch.

Carry your own water, and take your plastic back with you. The banks get messy after a busy Sunday.

Where to stay

There are a few stays along the river if you want to make a night of it. Four Banyans Spirit and Nature Resort, at Seraki, has tents on the Song river with meditation sessions and village walks. Maldevta Farms is a farm stay with breakfast. Prices change with the season, so check before you book.

Best time to visit Maldevta

October to March is the most comfortable. Winter afternoons are warm in the sun and the river is low. Summer mornings work if you leave early. In the monsoon (July to September) the valley is at its greenest, but paths get slippery and the river can rise without warning.

Combine it with

Sahastradhara is about 9 km away, so the two make an easy half day. Robber's Cave and Tapkeshwar Temple are on the other side of the city, so keep them for a different day.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Maldevta from Dehradun?

About 13 km from the Clock Tower and 18 km from ISBT, roughly 40 to 45 minutes by car.

Is there an entry fee at Maldevta?

No. It's an open riverside area. You only pay for food, parking where it's charged, or a stay.

Can you swim at Maldevta?

People paddle in the shallows, but the river rises quickly after rain. Stay out of the water in the monsoon and after any heavy shower in the hills.

What is the best time to visit Maldevta?

October to March. Weekday mornings are the quietest.
