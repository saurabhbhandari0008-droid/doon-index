Best Momos in Dehradun: Where to Eat Them, Area by Area

Chilli momos on Rajpur Road, kothey in Rajpur, a steel plate near Astley Hall and the evening carts around Ghanta Ghar.

Finding the best momos in Dehradun is less about one famous shop and more about knowing which kind you want. The city has sit-down Tibetan kitchens on Rajpur Road, Nepali places with their own chutneys, and carts that appear around the Clock Tower once the offices empty out.

This list is organised so you can find a good plate wherever you are in the city. It draws on the places that food writers and local lists keep naming.

Why momos are everywhere in Doon

Dehradun has a large Tibetan community, centred on Clement Town and the settlements around it, and a long connection with Nepal and the hills. Momos came with both. Over the years the city made them its own: you'll find fried, tandoori and kurkure versions next to the plain steamed ones, and almost every cart has its own red chutney.

The kinds you'll see on a menu

  • Steamed: the default, and the best test of a kitchen. Thin wrapper, juicy filling.
  • Fried: steamed first, then deep-fried until crisp.
  • Kothey: the Nepali style, pan-fried on one side so the base is crisp and the top stays soft.
  • Chilli momos: tossed in a thick, spicy gravy with onion and capsicum.
  • Tandoori and kurkure: the North Indian street versions. One is marinated and roasted, the other coated and fried crunchy.
  • Jhol: served in a thin, tangy soup.

Best momos in Dehradun: 8 places to try

1. Kalsang Friends Corner, Rajpur Road

Kalsang is the name most people give first. It's a Tibetan kitchen on Rajpur Road, and the chilli momos are what it's known for, in a thick gravy with peppers and shallots. Steamed momos, thukpa and thenthuk are on the menu too. On a cold evening, order a bowl of thenthuk alongside. Weekends get busy, so go early for dinner.

2. Tibet Kitchen – Didi Momo's, near New Empire Cinema

A small Tibetan, Chinese and Sikkimese kitchen on Rajpur Road, near the New Empire Cinema complex by Gandhi Park. It moved here from near the Clock Tower. It serves veg and non-veg momos and is a handy stop if you're shopping around Astley Hall or Gandhi Park.

3. Joshi Momos, near Astley Hall

A tiny stall that has been serving momos near Astley Hall for years. There's nothing fancy here: hot dumplings on a steel plate with sharp chutney, and they're cheap.

4. Orchards, Rajpur

Further up towards Rajpur, Orchards serves Tibetan and North-Eastern food with outdoor seating among the trees. The mutton and chicken kothey momos are the ones to order. One food list we read suggests skipping the cheese momos.

5. Thapa Restaurant, Hathibarkala

A no-fuss Tibetan place in Hathibarkala. The mutton momos are the reason to go: soft wrappers and a well-seasoned filling. Good for a quick lunch if you live in the Hathibarkala or Salawala area.

6. Saino, Chakrata Road

On the western side of the city, Saino serves Nepali food. The momos are plump and well seasoned, and they come with a strong tomato chutney that's different from the usual red chilli one. Worth the stop if you're near Chakrata Road or heading out towards Prem Nagar.

7. Singh Soup Bar, Rajpur Road

An open-air, drive-in place on Rajpur Road known for soups and momos with a fiery chilli sauce. Service is quick, which is why so many people stop here on the way home in the evening. Get a soup with your momos if it's cold.

8. The evening carts around Ghanta Ghar

The carts around the Ghanta Ghar (Clock Tower) and the edge of Paltan Bazaar are the classic place to eat momos. They come out in the evening. Pick the cart with the most people standing around it, and taste the chutney before you add more. If you're doing a bun tikki walk in Paltan Bazaar, finish here.

Momos by area

  • Rajpur Road and Astley Hall: Kalsang Friends Corner, Tibet Kitchen – Didi Momo's, Joshi Momos, Singh Soup Bar.
  • Rajpur and Hathibarkala: Orchards, Thapa Restaurant.
  • Chakrata Road: Saino.
  • Clock Tower and Paltan Bazaar: the evening carts.

How to tell a good plate of momos

The wrapper should be thin enough to see a little of the filling, and it shouldn't tear when you pick it up. A good veg filling tastes of cabbage and onion, not just oil. Chicken and mutton should be juicy, so a dry filling usually means the momos were steamed too long or reheated. The chutney matters as much as the momo. A good red chutney has garlic and tomato in it, not only chilli.

Local tip · if you can't handle much spice, ask for the chutney on the side. Some carts mix it in by default.

When to go

Momos are an evening food in Dehradun. The carts near the Clock Tower are a late-afternoon and evening thing, and winter evenings are when a hot plate makes the most sense. The sit-down places on Rajpur Road serve them at lunch too, so go then if you want a table without waiting.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find the best momos in Dehradun?

Kalsang Friends Corner on Rajpur Road is the name most people mention, especially for chilli momos. For street momos, try the evening carts around the Clock Tower or Joshi Momos near Astley Hall.

Which momos is Dehradun famous for?

Steamed Tibetan momos with red chutney are the classic. Chilli momos, fried momos and Nepali-style kothey are also easy to find.

Are there good vegetarian momos in Dehradun?

Yes. Veg momos, usually with a cabbage, onion and carrot filling, are on nearly every menu and cart in the city.

What time do momo carts open near the Clock Tower?

They're an evening thing. Most carts set up in the late afternoon and stay busy after office hours.
