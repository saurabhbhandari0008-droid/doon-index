Best Street Food in Dehradun: 10 Places Locals Swear By

From chole katlamba in Moti Bazaar to bun tikki in Dalanwala and momos at the Clock Tower: the street food in Dehradun that locals actually queue for.

Dehradun doesn't really advertise its food. There's no famous food street with neon boards and no celebrity stalls. The good stuff is in the narrow lanes behind the Clock Tower, in small shops that have been frying the same thing for decades, and in carts that only show up after 5 pm.

This is the list we'd give a friend visiting for the weekend. Go hungry, carry some cash along with UPI, and be ready to walk.

Why Dehradun street food tastes the way it does

A lot of the city's classic food came with Punjabi families who settled here after Partition. That's why chole katlamba, a very Punjabi breakfast, is the dish most people in Doon grew up on. Add the Tibetan community's momos, pahadi snacks and good old North Indian bun tikki, and you get a food scene that feels local but tastes of half of North India.

1. Sethi's Chole Katlambe, Moti Bazaar

If you try only one thing on this list, make it chole katlamba. A katlamba is a big, flaky, layered bread fried until crisp, served with mildly spiced chole. Sethi's, in the Moti Bazaar lanes near Paltan Bazaar, is one of the best-known names for it.

It's a breakfast and lunch place, so go in the morning. The shop is small and usually packed. That's part of it.

2. Mama Ji Katlambe Chole Wale, near Purani Sabzi Mandi

Ask anyone in Dehradun about katlamba and you'll eventually get the Sethi's versus Mama Ji argument. The only fair way to settle it is to try both. Mama Ji is in the lanes near the old vegetable market, close enough that you can walk from one to the other on the same morning.

3. Sethi Bun Tikki, Moti Bazaar

Bun tikki is Dehradun's favourite evening snack: a soft bun split open, a crisp aloo tikki inside, onion and green chutney. Simple, cheap, and very hard to eat just one. Sethi Bun Tikki is a short walk from the katlamba shops, so you can cover all of Moti Bazaar in one food walk.

4. Vicky Kashyap's Dwarka Bun Tikki, Dalanwala

Away from the old city, this stall near Dwarka Store in Dalanwala has its own loyal crowd. If you live on the eastern side of the city, it saves you a trip through Paltan Bazaar traffic.

5. Ratan Lal Khasta Chole, Hanuman Chowk

Khasta is crisp, flaky dough served with spicy chole, and Ratan Lal's near Hanuman Chowk has been a favourite for a long time. Pair it with a walk through the old bazaar.

6. Chetan Swadisht Kachori, near the railway station

Arriving or leaving by train? Hot kachori with sabzi near the station road is the most Dehradun way to start or end a trip.

7. Momos around the Clock Tower

Between the Tibetan community and how close we are to the hills, momos are everywhere in Doon. The evening carts around Ghanta Ghar and Paltan Bazaar are the classic place to eat them. Steamed is the default, fried is for when you've given up on the diet, and the red chutney decides whether a cart is good or not.

8. Tibetan food on Rajpur Road

If you want to sit down, the Tibetan kitchens along Rajpur Road serve thukpa, thenthuk and momos. Kalsang is the best-known name, and a bowl of thukpa there on a cold evening is hard to beat.

9. Maggi and pakoda by the river

A very Dehradun weekend plan: drive out to Sahastradhara or Maldevta and eat masala Maggi, bread pakoda and cutting chai at a riverside stall. Honestly, you're there for the river more than the food, and that's fine.

10. Bakery stops for rusks and cheese straws

Not street food exactly, but no Dehradun food walk is complete without a bakery stop. The city is famous for its rusks, and the old bakeries on Rajpur Road and around town still sell them by the box. It's the best edible souvenir you can take home. More in our guide to bakeries in Dehradun.

A simple street food walk

Start in the morning at the Moti Bazaar katlamba shops. If you still have room, walk over to Ratan Lal for khasta chole. Spend the afternoon shopping in Paltan Bazaar (our shopping guide has the details), then come back in the evening for bun tikki and momos around the Clock Tower. Finish with chai.

Tips before you go

  • Go early for breakfast dishes. Katlamba and chole shops often sell out or shut by afternoon.
  • Park outside the old city and walk in. The lanes are narrow and parking is a headache.
  • Most shops take UPI now, but keep some cash for the smaller carts.
  • Not used to spice? Ask for the chutney on the side.
  • Weekends are crowded. Weekday mornings are much calmer.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most famous street food of Dehradun?

Chole katlamba and bun tikki are the two dishes locals mention most, followed closely by momos.

Where is the best area for street food in Dehradun?

The lanes around Paltan Bazaar, Moti Bazaar and the Clock Tower have the most classic street food in one place.

Is Dehradun street food good for vegetarians?

Yes. Most of the classics, including katlamba, bun tikki, kachori and khasta chole, are vegetarian.

What time should I go for street food in Dehradun?

Mornings for chole katlamba and kachori. Evenings for bun tikki and momos.
