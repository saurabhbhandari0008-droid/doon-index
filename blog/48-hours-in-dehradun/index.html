48 hours in Dehradun

A weekend plan: bakeries for breakfast, a river gorge before lunch, momos by the Clock Tower and the view from the Mussoorie road at sunset.

You can cross Dehradun in half an hour, but most of what's worth seeing sits just off the main roads: a river gorge, a monastery, a bakery that has made the same rusks for decades. This is how we'd fit it into two days.

Day one: the city

Start on Rajpur Road with tea and something baked. Walk north while the road is still quiet, then take an auto to the Forest Research Institute. Give the campus at least two hours. Everyone photographs the main building, but the walks between the museums are what most people end up talking about.

Head back towards the Clock Tower for a late lunch, then spend the afternoon in Paltan Bazaar. Buy basmati and woollens, eat a bun-tikki, and finish with momos from one of the carts near Ghanta Ghar.

Leave room in the plan. The best stops are rarely on the main road.

Day two: water and hills

Leave early for Robber’s Cave. By 8 am you will have the gorge almost to yourself. On the way back, stop at Tapkeshwar Temple, then rest through the afternoon in a Rajpur Road café.

Before dark, drive up the Mussoorie road for the view back over the valley. On a clear evening the whole city is lit up below you.

Local tip · Plan the hills for the morning after rain. The air is clearest then and the views go all the way to the plains.
