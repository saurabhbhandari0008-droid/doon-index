Sahastradhara, Dehradun: Sulphur Springs, Caves and the Ropeway

The 'thousand-fold spring' 14 km from the city: what's actually there, how to reach it and how to avoid the weekend crush.

Sahastradhara is Dehradun's most searched place to visit, and also its most crowded on a Sunday. The name means 'thousand-fold spring'. Water seeps out of limestone overhangs along the Baldi river and drips down in hundreds of thin streams, and the springs here carry sulphur.

It's about 14 km from the city, according to the Dehradun Nagar Nigam, along Sahastradhara Road. Plan on half a day.

What there is to do at Sahastradhara

The dripping caves

The main sight is a row of shallow caves and rock shelves where water drips constantly from the ceiling. It's what gives the place its name. The rock is slippery, so go slowly.

The sulphur springs

The river below the caves has pools fed by sulphur springs. Many visitors bathe in them because sulphur water has long been believed to be good for the skin. Whether or not it does anything, the water is cold and the pools get crowded by late morning.

The ropeway

There is also a ropeway with views over the valley. It is ticketed separately, and prices change, so check at the counter.

Rides and the river stalls

Around the springs there are small amusement rides and a line of stalls selling Maggi, pakoras, corn and chai. Seeing the springs and caves is free; the ropeway and rides cost extra.

How to reach Sahastradhara from Dehradun

Take Sahastradhara Road east from the city, past the IT Park. By car or bike it takes 30 to 45 minutes from the Clock Tower depending on traffic. Autos and cabs will take you there; agree the return fare before you set off if you want them to wait.

Parking near the springs fills up on weekends and holidays, so arrive early.

When to go

  • Before noon on a weekday is the calmest time.
  • Summer (April to June) is when the water feels best and the crowds are biggest.
  • In the monsoon the river rises fast. Stay out of the water after heavy rain and follow any closures.
  • In winter the springs are quiet and cold. The caves are still worth the drive.

What to carry

  • Footwear that can get wet. The rocks around the caves are always damp.
  • A change of clothes and a towel if you plan to get into the pools.
  • A bag for your wet things and any rubbish. Bins are few.

If you want a quieter river spot, Maldevta in the Raipur area is a good alternative. Robber's Cave (Guchhupani) is on the other side of the city, so it's easier on a separate morning.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Sahastradhara from Dehradun?

About 14 km from the city, along Sahastradhara Road. Allow 30 to 45 minutes from the Clock Tower.

Is there an entry fee at Sahastradhara?

It's free to see the springs and caves. The ropeway and amusement rides are ticketed separately.

Can you bathe at Sahastradhara?

Yes, many visitors bathe in the sulphur pools. Avoid the river after heavy rain, when the water rises quickly.

What is the best time to visit Sahastradhara?

A weekday morning. Weekends and summer holidays are very crowded by midday.
